04 — Our recommendation

Direction A, with B’s nerve.

“All Bread, No Cheese” is the only route that turns the low price into proof of good judgement rather than a compromise. We’d borrow B’s bold price-forward headlines for social, and C’s calm product-grade interface details.

01
It feels established on day one.
Editorial serif, broadsheet rules and field-guide captions borrow the authority of publishing and heritage brands. Customers read “safe pair of hands” before they read a word.
02
The idea is ownable, and true.
The bird’s own song gives us a line no other agency can claim. “No cheese” quietly rejects the template look your customers fear when they see a low price.
03
It speaks to who actually buys.
Cafés, trades, clinics, salons and independent shops want to feel proud of their brand, not lectured by a tech company. A is warm and British where C is cool and SaaS-like.
04
It stretches without breaking.
Plates, figures and field notes give us an endless, consistent format for case studies, blog posts and social, so a small team can publish a lot without it looking cheap.
Our assessment
A B C Feels premium ●●● ●●○ ●●● Feels established ●●● ●○○ ●●○ Memorable & ownable ●●● ●●● ●●○ Small-business appeal ●●● ●●○ ●○○ Social-first stretch ●●○ ●●● ●●○ Ages well (3–5 yrs) ●●● ●○○ ●●○

B is the most memorable, but black-and-yellow reads as hazard tape or discount retail, and shouting the price risks making it the whole story. C is beautiful, but dark and techy for local owners, and closest to what many AI-built sites already look like.
